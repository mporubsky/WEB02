The Kindergarten Program

Each child needs a program designed to help them achieve their maximum potential

We respect The Kindergarten Program as an open document providing specific expectations for children’s learning in kindergarten. It outlines the knowledge and skills children should develop by the end of kindergarten. These, along with the positive attitudes to learning that children develop in these early years, form the basis of effective learning in the later years of school.

Teachers adapt programs for children with special needs. If necessary they discuss appropriate adaptations with parents, and make sure that parents understand how these adaptations may affect the assessment and evaluation of the child’s achievement. Parents are also asked to share with teachers any special adjustments and arrangements that have been helpful for their children in the past.

Teachers, early-childhood educators, members of the community and families must work together to provide constructive and consistent learning experiences that build children’s confidence, encourage them to see learning as both enjoyable and useful, and provide a strong foundation for their future intellectual, physical and social development.

Integrated learning

Children grasp ideas more easily and maintain their interest when the program is integrated — one that lets them connect their learning to their own lives and the world around them, and helps them see how knowledge and skills in one area are relevant to others.

Learning through inquiry

Most young children are naturally curious. Our program capitalises on this desire to learn: children have many opportunities to manipulate materials, observe, listen, investigate, experiment, ask questions and draw conclusions. To encourage full use of those opportunities, the learning environment needs to be safe, secure and inviting.

Learning through play

There is a strong link between play and learning for young children, especially in problem solving, literacy and social skills. Play therefore has a legitimate and important role, and is used to further children’s learning in all areas of the program. During play with others and with materials in their environment, children become immersed in activities through which they learn about themselves and explore their world.
